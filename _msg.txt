feat(skill): correct the eye doctrine -- solid blocks stacked by depth, not painted texture

The previous entry claimed eye geometry barely matters and expression lives in the
texture. That was wrong, and the corpus says so plainly.

Measured across 157 models with eye geometry: the median colour standard deviation of an
eye block's texture region is 0.00 (p75 also 0), and 82% of eye blocks sit below 2. In
other words the texture holds flat swatches, not a painted eye. Composition over the whole
corpus: eyebrow 849 blocks, misc eye parts 824, eye base 358, pupil 322, highlight 270,
eyelid plate 238.

Median dimensions per part: eyelid plate / eye white 2.00 x 1.52 x 1.00, iris 0.9-1.2 x
1.0-1.3, pupil 0.40 x 0.50 x 1.00, eye base 1.20 x 0.50, highlight 0.50 x 0.40 x 0.05
(ultra thin), eyebrow 1.15 x 0.50.

The reason is animation: eyes must blink, rotate and emote. A painted texture cannot be
animated, blocks can be moved, rotated and hidden independently -- and high-resolution
textures are expensive in YSM, so the texture budget should not be spent on the eye.

Section 3.1.4 rewritten around the correct build order, back to front: eyelid plate, iris,
pupil, one or two ultra-thin highlights offset to the upper left, an eye-base strip over
the upper rim that produces the lash shadow, then a three-block tapering eyebrow. Depth
separation plus occlusion is what makes the eye look set into its socket. Blink animation
works by moving the iris and highlight group while the lid plate stays as a mask.

Also records the two anti-patterns explicitly: painting a full eye on a high-res texture
and using one flat plate, and micro-sculpting 0.2-thick pupils. And it requires eye checks
to use isolate at normal viewing scale rather than only at 8x zoom.
